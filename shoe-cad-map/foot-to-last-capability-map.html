2 · Five ways to get from a foot to a last

The tools differ less in raw power than in which of these routes they implement. Pick the route first, the tool second.

A

Direct scan → last

Import the foot mesh, apply allowances, let the tool derive the last.

3DShoemaker · Shoemaster · PROTIQ · Materialise

Verdict: Fastest path to a genuinely custom shape. Demands a decent scan; a bad scan becomes a bad last with no warning.

B

Base last → morph to the foot

Start from a house last and deform it region by region toward the scan.

Shoemaster · 3DShoemaker (morph) · Rhino/Grasshopper · nTop

Verdict: Keeps the styling, toe shape and heel seat of a proven last; only fit-critical zones move. Best route for a wearable, good-looking shoe.

C

Measurements → parametric last

Length, ball girth, instep, heel width drive a parametric last model.

Open Shoe Designer · ICad3D+ · Romans CAD · Crispin · OpenSCAD

Verdict: No scanner needed and fully reproducible, but you inherit the average shape behind the numbers. Weakest on unusual feet.

D

Scan → surface / insole only

Skip the last; build the interface layer that actually touches the foot.

Gensole · Materialise Phits · IcadPAN · nTop

Verdict: Highest comfort return per hour of work. Does not give you a shoe — you still need an upper and an outsole.

E

Scan → service prints it

You supply the capture; the vendor owns the whole design pipeline.

Shoefactory.ai · Fitasy · FitStation · PROTIQ print service

Verdict: Zero CAD skill required. You get an artefact, not a model — no parametric control, no reusable last, limited styles.

Last core weights only the features that decide whether a tool can turn a foot into a printable last (scan import ×3, last generation ×3, allowances ×3, parametric control ×3, AM export ×3, plus measurement, morphing, sculpting, clinical correction, deviation comparison and print checks). Cells rated ? are excluded from both numerator and denominator, so a score never claims credit for an unconfirmed feature. Best last-core: Shoemaster · best full-shoe: Shoemaster.

7 · Reading the map: what actually decides fit

Six features carry almost all of the comfort outcome. Everything else is convenience.

Allowancesmake or break
A foot is not a last. Toe allowance, fit allowance, sock thickness and heel-seat grip are the offsets that turn a scan into something wearable. Tools without them give you a plaster cast of your foot, which is unwearable.
Weight-bearing statesystematically ignored
A foot spreads roughly 4–10 mm in length and more in girth under load. A non-weight-bearing scan produces a tight shoe. Almost no tool in this map documents how it handles the difference — that is why so many cells read ?.
Deviation comparisonyour only feedback loop
A colour map of last-vs-foot tells you where the shoe will press. Without it you are iterating blind, printing and re-printing. Shoemaster, nTop, Materialise and CloudCompare all provide a version of this.
Morphing vs rebuildingthe styling trap
Deriving a last straight from a scan loses the toe shape, heel seat and lines of a real shoe. Morphing a proven base last toward the foot keeps the design and moves only the fit-critical zones.
Parametric controliterability
Fit is found by iteration. If changing ball girth by 2 mm means re-sculpting the whole surface, you will stop iterating — and you will stop at a bad fit. History-based rebuild is what makes 20 attempts feasible.
AM exportthe quiet blocker
Several professional footwear CAD packages export proprietary or NURBS-native data aimed at CNC and cutting, not at a slicer. Confirm STL/3MF/AMF in millimetres before buying. This is the most common dead end.
